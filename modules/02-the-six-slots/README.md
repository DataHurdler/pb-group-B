# Module 02 — The Six Slots

Status: draft
Time: 10:00–11:00 (60 min) · Objective(s): LO2

## Why this module, here

Module 01 showed that the AI fills in whatever you leave out. This module
gives participants a checklist of what to put in, so they stop leaving
holes. Module 03 applies it straight away to executive writing.

## By the end, participants can

- Write a prompt that fills all six slots (LO2).
- Look at a weak output, name the empty slot, and fill it (LO2).

## Teach: the six slots

| Slot | The question it answers | Failure when empty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task** | What exactly do you want produced? | It does something adjacent |
| **Context** | What does it need to know that it can't? | Generic, made-up details |
| **Audience** | Who will read it, and what do they care about? | Wrong tone and depth |
| **Format** | Length, structure, headings, bullets? | A wall of text, or the wrong product |
| **Constraints** | What must it not do? What must it stick to? | Invented facts, banned phrasing |
| **Example** | What does good look like? | It guesses at a style you then have to fix |

Two habits to finish:

- **Paste the source and say "use only this."** This is the strongest guard
  against invented facts. It's taught here and checked again in Module 05.
- **Ask it to ask you.** End with *"Before you write, ask me any questions
  you need answered."* It shows which slot you left empty.

## Instructor notes

- **Name the failure before the fix.** Show the generic output first, then the
  slot that fixes it.
- **Skip "act as a world-class expert" folklore.** A role line helps a little.
  Context and audience help a lot.
- **If running long, cut** the Lab 02 stretch task.
