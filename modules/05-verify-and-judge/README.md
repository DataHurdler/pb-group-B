# Module 05 — Verify and Judge

Status: draft
Time: 2:00–2:50 (50 min) · Objective(s): LO5

## Why this module, here

Everything so far makes AI output faster and better-shaped, and that also
makes errors more convincing. One wrong number in a memo to a general officer
costs more than the time AI saved. This module is the last thing taught
before the assessment, which weights accuracy most heavily.

## By the end, participants can

- Check every fact in an AI draft against its source, and catch invented
  numbers, dates, and authorities (LO5).
- Decide whether a task should use AI at all, and give the reason (LO5).

## Teach

**How AI output goes wrong:**

| Failure | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Changed number | 78% becomes 87% |
| Invented fact | A cost the source never mentions |
| Invented authority | "Per DoD policy…" with no source |
| Lost caveat | An estimate presented as data |

**Defences:**

1. **"Use only the text I provided. If it isn't there, say 'not in source'."**
2. **"After each claim, quote the source line it came from."** If there's no
   quote, it isn't a fact.
3. **Check every number, date, and name by hand.** It's slow, but it's the
   job, and it's what you sign for.
4. **Ask a fresh conversation to critique it.** "List every claim in this
   summary that the source text doesn't support."

**When not to use AI:**

- Anything the approved tool isn't authorized to hold.
- Decisions about people: evaluations, discipline, selection.
- Final legal, policy, or contracting interpretations. AI can draft
  questions for the expert, not the answer.
- Anything you can't check against a source.
- Anything where how it was produced matters as much as the result.
  AI conversations may also be federal records, so ask your records manager.
